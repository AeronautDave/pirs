How to do a PIR.

Turn an implemented recommendation into a clear, evidence-led story of what changed, what happened and what we should do next.

PIR

Simple in structure.
Valuable in practice.

Use this 15-minute training aid to learn the method, work through an example and test your judgement.

01 / Definition

One change.
One evidence trail.

A Post Implementation Review is a performance review of an individual change or recommendation. It communicates what we did, why we did it and what it achieved.

A PIR is

  • ✓Tied to a specific deliverable or implemented change
  • ✓Measured against a clear opportunity, hypothesis or intended outcome
  • ✓A client-ready story with results and learning

A PIR is not

  • ×A monthly report with activity totals
  • ×Something constrained to month-end or quarterly cycles
  • ×A task to ignore once implementation is complete
02 / Workflow

The PIR
process.

The review starts when the work is delivered, not when someone remembers to look back. Capture the implementation date and schedule the evidence review at the right interval.

01

Deliverable

Create the recommendation or deliverable and send it to the client.

02

Implemented

Confirm the change is live on the site or platform.

03

Notification

Record the completion date so the measurement window is reliable.

04

Schedule

Create a Teamwork task for the point at which meaningful evidence should exist.

05

PIR

Review the evidence and communicate it by email, deck or another suitable format.

03 / Narrative

Four questions.
One useful story.

Keep the completed review anchored to four questions. The result should make the commercial value and the next decision obvious.

01

What was the opportunity?

State the problem, baseline and intended outcome.

02

What did we do?

Describe the implemented change precisely, including timing.

03

What were the results?

Compare like with like and show evidence against the intended outcome.

04

What did we learn?

Explain what the evidence changes about the next action or recommendation.

04 / Practice

Build the
story.

Use the brief to draft a concise PIR. Reveal the model only after you have made your own judgement.

Scenario

Category template optimisation

Opportunity
Strong impressions, but low non-brand CTR on high-value category pages.
Implementation
Titles and descriptions rewritten on 12 category pages, live 8 September.
Evidence after 8 weeks
Non-brand clicks +18%, impressions +4%, CTR from 2.7% to 3.1%. No material ranking change.
Model response

We identified underperforming CTR across 12 commercially important category pages despite strong visibility. Revised metadata went live on 8 September. Over the following eight weeks, non-brand clicks increased 18% as impressions rose 4% and CTR improved from 2.7% to 3.1%, without a material ranking change. This suggests the uplift was driven mainly by stronger search-result messaging. Next, apply the approach to a matched test group and monitor whether the gain persists.

05 / Check

Test your
judgement.

Choose the best answer. The aim is not recall alone, but knowing when a review is decision-ready.

1. When should the PIR task be scheduled?

2. Which result is most useful in a PIR?

3. What makes the learning section valuable?

Make the value
visible.

PIRs create a steady flow of proof, strengthen learning and give clients clear reasons to continue investing. The discipline is simple: record the change, choose the right review window and connect the evidence to the next decision.
