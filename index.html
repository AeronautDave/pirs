01 / Definition

one change.
one evidence trail.

A Post Implementation Review is a performance review of an individual change or recommendation. It communicates what we did, why we did it and what it achieved.

A PIR is

  • ✓Tied to a specific deliverable or implemented change
  • ✓Measured against a clear opportunity, hypothesis or intended outcome
  • ✓A client-ready story with results and learning

A PIR is not

  • ×A monthly report with activity totals
  • ×Something constrained to month-end or quarterly cycles
  • ×A task to ignore once implementation is complete
02 / Workflow

the PIR
process.

The review starts when the work is delivered, not when someone remembers to look back. Capture the implementation date and schedule the evidence review at the right interval.

01

Deliverable

Create the recommendation or deliverable and send it to the client.

02

Implemented

Confirm the change is live on the site or platform.

03

Notification

Record the completion date so the measurement window is reliable.

04

Schedule

Create a Teamwork task for the point at which meaningful evidence should exist.

05

PIR

Review the evidence and communicate it by email, deck or another suitable format.

03 / Narrative

four questions.
one useful story.

Keep the completed review anchored to four questions. The result should make the commercial value and the next decision obvious.

01

What was the opportunity?

State the problem, baseline and intended outcome.

02

What did we do?

Describe the implemented change precisely, including timing.

03

What were the results?

Compare like with like and show evidence against the intended outcome.

04

What did we learn?

Explain what the evidence changes about the next action or recommendation.

04 / Examples

what good
looks like.

Each example connects one implemented change to an evidence window, a result and a clear next decision. Notice that the response is concise, specific and honest about what the evidence can support.

SEO example

Category template optimisation

Improving non-brand CTR across commercially important category pages.

Example PIR

We identified underperforming CTR across 12 commercially important category pages despite strong visibility. Revised metadata went live on 8 September. Over the following eight weeks, non-brand clicks increased 18% as impressions rose 4% and CTR improved from 2.7% to 3.1%, without a material ranking change. This suggests the uplift was driven mainly by stronger search-result messaging. Next, apply the approach to a matched test group and monitor whether the gain persists.

Content example

Buying-guide refresh

Testing whether stronger intent alignment would improve organic engagement and product discovery.

Example PIR

Search Console and on-page behaviour showed that our existing sofa buying guide attracted research traffic but generated limited progression to product pages. We restructured the guide around common buying decisions, added comparison content and introduced contextual links to relevant categories on 14 August. During the following ten weeks, non-brand clicks increased 27% and visits from the guide to product categories rose 34%, while average organic position remained broadly stable. The evidence indicates that better intent coverage and internal journeys improved both search demand capture and onward engagement. We should now apply the same structure to the two highest-opportunity buying guides and track assisted conversions as the next success measure.

Digital PR example

Data-led campaign

Assessing whether a focused campaign strengthened authority around a priority topic.

Example PIR

Competitor analysis identified an authority gap around sustainable home improvements, a priority topic for the client. We launched a data-led campaign on 3 June using regional research and expert commentary. The campaign secured 31 referring domains, including nine publications with a Domain Rating above 70. Across the following three months, the linked resource gained 42% more organic impressions and moved from an average position of 18.6 to 11.9 for the target query set. Rankings cannot be attributed to links alone, but the timing and topic relevance support continuing this approach. The next campaign should retain the regional data format while building stronger links into the related commercial hub.
